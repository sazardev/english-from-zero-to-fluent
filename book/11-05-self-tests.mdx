# Self-tests

Four tests, one per stage of the book. Take them closed book, timed, and
mark them honestly. The point is not the score; it is finding out what to
read next.

Mark each answer right or wrong. No marks for a nearly right answer.

1. ___ apple a day.
2. She has two ___, a cat and a dog.
3. This is the book ___ I told you about.
4. He has lived here ___ 2019.
5. I saw him ___ night.
6. There ___ no milk in the fridge.
7. ___ is important to be honest.
8. The dog wagged ___ tail.
9. She is ___ teacher.
10. I am interested ___ history.
11. Do you know where ___ ?
12. This is the man ___ car was stolen.
13. He depends ___ his brother.
14. It consists ___ three parts.
15. I am good ___ maths.
16. She gave me some useful ___ .
17. He is ___ than his brother.
18. I have ___ worked here for a year.
19. They ___ in Berlin last year.
20. The film ___ interesting.

**19-20 A2. 16-18 strong A2. 13-15 A2 with gaps. Below 13, start at part 1
and do it again.**

## Test 3: the sentence, B2

Fifteen questions, fifteen minutes. These are about structure rather than
form.

1. Put in the right connector: *He was tired, ___ he kept going.*
2. Same: *___ he was tired, he kept going.*
3. Same: *___ it was raining, we went for a walk.*
4. Same: *___ the rain, we went for a walk.*
5. Choose: *The problem is ___ matter ___ timing.*
6. Complete: *It was John ___ told me.*
7. Complete: *It is the timing ___ is the problem.*
8. Reduce the clause: *the man who was standing there*
9. Reduce the clause: *the report which was written in 2019*
10. Reduce the clause: *the students who are taking the exam*
11. Write a cleft: *I need more time.*
12. Choose: *I was tired, ___ I went to bed.*
13. Rewrite with inversion: *I had never seen such a thing.*
14. Correct: *Never I had seen such a thing.*
15. Correct: *Although it was long, but I enjoyed it.*

**15 all correct B2. 12-14 upper B1. Below 12, part 5.**

## Test 4: writing, C1

Four tasks, forty-five minutes. This is the one that tells you something
real, because the earlier tests can be passed by recognising patterns.

1. **A paragraph, 120 words.** Argue that remote work is worse for a
   company. Topic sentence, development, closing.
2. **A summary, 60 words.** Summarise the argument in part 8.6, the
   fourteen errors, in your own words, without reusing its phrasing.
3. **A rewrite.** Rewrite this in academic register: *The stuff we found
   shows that the old way of doing it doesn't really work, but we're not
   sure why yet, because we only did it once.*
4. **Error hunt.** Find and correct every error in this paragraph.

> He don't know nothing about the result of the experiment. The samples
> was tested two time, and the first one has been contaminate. Because of
> this, the informations are not reliable. Although the team work hard,
> they didn't have enough time. If they would have had more time, they
> would have get better result. Its possible that the problem are in the
> method and not in the samples, and this is what we think to be the most
> likely explanation.

**Score yourself: one point per error found. There are fourteen, and they
are all on the list in part 8.6 except the two that are not: a double
negative and a second conditional.**

## Reading the scores

| result | what it means |
|---|---|
| 19+ on test 1 | move to part 2 |
| 15+ on test 2 | move to part 5 |
| all of test 3 | move to parts 6 to 8 |
| under 18 on test 4 | read part 8.6 again and keep the correction log |
| under 14 on test 4 | the errors are not yet automatic; slow down the grammar and speed up the deck |

<Warning>
The most useful number in this chapter is not any of these scores. It is
the one you get if you take a test, then work through the chapter, then
take it again in a month.

A test measures a moment. What you want to know is whether the moment is
moving, and only two tests separated by a month can tell you. Almost every
learner who works through part 8.6 once scores badly, takes it again after
a month of practice and scores well, and the only thing that changed was
the number of times they had seen those fourteen errors.

This is also the argument for the correction log, stated as a measurement
rather than as advice. The log is what turns a test score from a fact
about your memory into a trend, and a trend is what tells you whether the
method is working.
</Warning>

## Timing yourself honestly

The times above are part of the test, not a convenience. A test taken
without a time limit measures recognition, and recognition is a much
easier thing to do than production.

If you finish test 1 in five minutes rather than ten, you probably
recognised the patterns. Try it again in twenty minutes, writing full
sentences instead of single words, and see whether the score survives.

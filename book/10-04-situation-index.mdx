# Index of situations

Which verb fits which situation is a judgement about meaning, not a fact
anybody has tabulated, which is why no dictionary can give it to you.
These are the situations that speakers confuse with each other, and the
tense and pattern each one uses.

## Making a decision

A decision taken at the moment of speaking. Not a plan made earlier.

**Form:** present simple
**Frame:** `I will {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `decide`, `choose`, `pick`, `take`, `go with`, `settle on`, `go for`

> I'll take the bus.
> We've decided to stay at home.
> I always go for the cheapest option.

## An intention made in advance

A prior arrangement, not a live decision. Using the present simple here is one of the classic B1 mistakes.

**Form:** going to / present continuous
**Frame:** `I am going to {v}.`

**Verbs that fit:** `meet`, `travel`, `see`, `call`, `have lunch`, `visit`, `move`, `get married`

> I'm meeting Ana at seven.
> We're going to travel in August.
> She is seeing her sister tomorrow.

## A fixed arrangement with someone else

The other person already agreed. Both the plan and the arrangement use the same two forms.

**Form:** present continuous
**Frame:** `I'm {v}ing with them.`

**Verbs that fit:** `meet`, `have lunch`, `see`, `stay`, `work`, `travel`, `study`, `play`

> I'm having lunch with Marco.
> We're staying at a hotel.
> They're playing tennis on Sunday.

## Something that happens regularly

A repeated or usual thing, not a one-off.

**Form:** present simple
**Frame:** `I {v} every day.`

**Verbs that fit:** `work`, `study`, `live`, `get up`, `have breakfast`, `commute`, `train`, `read`

> I study English every morning.
> She works from home on Fridays.
> We train three times a week.

## Routines, especially with the present continuous

Repeated actions can take the continuous: it sounds more like a routine and less like a fact.

**Form:** present continuous
**Frame:** `I'm usually {v}ing at this time.`

**Verbs that fit:** `work`, `travel`, `commute`, `have lunch`, `drive`, `fly`

> I'm usually driving at this hour.
> I'm having lunch at one.

## A finished action at a definite time

The time is stated or understood. Past simple, not present perfect.

**Form:** past simple
**Frame:** `Yesterday I {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `finish`, `call`, `meet`, `send`, `buy`, `leave`, `arrive`, `see`

> I finished the report yesterday.
> We met at the airport.
> She sent the email this morning.

## A finished action, time not stated

Past actions with present relevance. The definite time is missing, which is exactly the condition for the perfect.

**Form:** present perfect
**Frame:** `I have already {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `finish`, `read`, `write`, `see`, `do`, `buy`, `lose`, `find`, `break`

> I have already finished it.
> Have you ever been to Japan?
> She has lost her keys.

## A past situation that is still true now

Not an action. A state, an ownership, a job, a home. Present perfect, never past simple with 'for' or 'since'.

**Form:** present perfect
**Frame:** `I have {v} here for three years.`

**Verbs that fit:** `live`, `work`, `study`, `teach`, `own`, `know`, `use`, `belong`

> I have lived here for ten years.
> She has worked here since 2019.
> We have known each other for years.

## An action started in the past and still running

Not finished. For or since + a point in time.

**Form:** present perfect continuous
**Frame:** `I have been {v}ing all morning.`

**Verbs that fit:** `work`, `study`, `read`, `wait`, `live`, `drive`, `learn`, `use`

> I have been working here for five years.
> She has been studying all morning.
> We have been waiting for an hour.

## A plan with a time set

With a specific time, use the present simple. Without one, use will or going to.

**Form:** will / going to
**Frame:** `I will {v} it tomorrow.`

**Verbs that fit:** `call`, `see`, `finish`, `start`, `bring`, `send`, `meet`

> I will call you tomorrow.
> We're going to start at nine.

## A prediction about the present

No time reference. Speaking about now, not about a plan.

**Form:** will
**Frame:** `He will {v} it now, I think.`

**Verbs that fit:** `be`, `use`, `have`, `need`, `seem`, `look`

> He will be at home now.
> That won't take long.

## A decision made while speaking

Something decided on the spot. Going to would be wrong: no plan existed before you opened your mouth.

**Form:** will
**Frame:** `I'll have the soup, please.`

**Verbs that fit:** `have`, `get`, `take`, `do`, `send`, `help`, `ask`

> I'll have the soup.
> I'll help with that.
> I'll ask him tomorrow.

## An offer or a request

The same structure as spontaneous decisions, used to help or to ask for something.

**Form:** will
**Frame:** `Will you {v} it?`

**Verbs that fit:** `help`, `carry`, `open`, `check`, `wait`, `come`, `pass`, `mind`

> Will you help me?
> Will you pass the salt?
> Would you mind waiting?

## A polite request

Present is softer than the past, which reads as a statement about what already happened.

**Form:** present simple
**Frame:** `Could you {v} it, please?`

**Verbs that fit:** `close`, `turn off`, `move`, `hold`, `send`, `sign`, `repeat`

> Could you close the window?
> Could you repeat that, please?

## An obligation

An external rule, from a person or an institution. Not your own feeling.

**Form:** must / have to
**Frame:** `I have to {v} it by Friday.`

**Verbs that fit:** `submit`, `pay`, `arrive`, `finish`, `renew`, `attend`, `return`, `register`

> I have to submit it by Friday.
> You must wear a helmet here.

## A recommendation, someone's opinion

A judgement, from a person. If it is your own advice, 'should' works too.

**Form:** should / ought to
**Frame:** `You should {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `see`, `ask`, `check`, `leave`, `take`, `call`, `find`, `rest`

> You should see a doctor.
> You ought to leave earlier.

## Suggesting an option

Presenting a possibility, not giving an instruction.

**Form:** could / should / how about
**Frame:** `Why don't you {v} it?`

**Verbs that fit:** `try`, `call`, `ask`, `use`, `wait`, `check`, `ask for`

> Why don't you try again?
> You could ask for a refund.

## Asking or giving permission

'Can' is the informal one. 'May' is more formal.

**Form:** can / may / could
**Frame:** `Can I {v} it?`

**Verbs that fit:** `open`, `use`, `sit`, `borrow`, `take`, `leave`, `park`, `enter`

> Can I open the window?
> May I use your pen?

## What is possible

A chance, not a plan. The context tells you which.

**Form:** may / might / could
**Frame:** `It might {v} later.`

**Verbs that fit:** `rain`, `happen`, `change`, `cost`, `take`, `last`, `work`

> It might rain later.
> He could be right.

## Something you did not do

Blame or regret, in the past. The perfect is the only option.

**Form:** should have + past participle
**Frame:** `I should have {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `tell`, `ask`, `check`, `book`, `call`, `read`, `buy`, `leave`

> I should have told you.
> You should have asked first.

## A mistake, or a missed opportunity

A regret about a specific past action.

**Form:** could have / would have
**Frame:** `I could have {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `been`, `gone`, `said`, `done`, `known`, `seen`, `helped`, `used`

> I could have been there.
> You would have known if you'd asked.

## A condition that is always true

Present on both sides. If this happens, that happens, always.

**Form:** present simple
**Frame:** `If I {v}, I feel sick.`

**Verbs that fit:** `eat`, `see`, `drink`, `smoke`, `hear`, `touch`

> If I eat seafood, I feel sick.
> If it rains, we stay in.

## A real future possibility

A real and likely condition. The result is more certain than the condition.

**Form:** present simple + will
**Frame:** `If I {v} it, I will call you.`

**Verbs that fit:** `finish`, `find`, `see`, `get`, `win`, `finish early`, `decide`, `remember`

> If I finish early, I'll call you.
> If you see her, tell her.

## An unreal or unlikely present situation

Imagined, or politely untrue. 'were' with every person, not 'was'.

**Form:** past simple + would
**Frame:** `If I {v} more time, I would learn it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `have`, `be`, `know`, `live`, `win`, `get`, `speak`

> If I had more time, I would learn it.
> If I were you, I would take the job.

## An unreal past situation

Talking about the past, and the result did not happen.

**Form:** had + past participle + would have
**Frame:** `If I had {v} it, I would have passed.`

**Verbs that fit:** `study`, `ask`, `leave`, `know`, `take`, `try`, `read`

> If I had studied, I would have passed.
> If she had asked, I would have helped.

## Reporting what someone said

The reporting verb decides the tense back, plus the pronoun and the time expression all move.

**Form:** reported speech
**Frame:** `He said (that) he {v}ed it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `work`, `live`, `study`, `have`, `go`, `be`, `know`, `want`

> He said he worked there.
> She said she was leaving.

## Acting on an object, not naming the doer

The subject is the thing acted on. Use it when the doer is unknown or obvious.

**Form:** passive
**Frame:** `It was {v} yesterday.`

**Verbs that fit:** `build`, `write`, `discover`, `break`, `repair`, `cancel`, `invite`, `award`

> It was built in 1850.
> The email was sent this morning.

## Stative verbs with no continuous form

A state, not an action. The continuous is ungrammatical.

**Form:** present simple only
**Frame:** `I {v} him.`

**Verbs that fit:** `know`, `like`, `love`, `hate`, `belong`, `own`, `need`, `want`, `seem`, `contain`, `consist`, `matter`, `believe`

> I know the answer.
> She likes coffee.
> This belongs to me.

## A deduction about the past

A conclusion, not a fact. The perfect is required.

**Form:** must / might / can't have + past participle
**Frame:** `He must have {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `forget`, `lose`, `leave`, `finish`, `buy`, `break`, `meet`, `see`

> He must have forgotten.
> They can't have left without us.

## Looking back from a point in the future

Finished before another future moment.

**Form:** will have + past participle
**Frame:** `By then I will have {v} it.`

**Verbs that fit:** `finish`, `read`, `write`, `complete`, `learn`, `move`, `graduate`

> By June I will have finished it.
> By then you will have moved.

## Two past actions in sequence

The earlier one takes the past perfect, the later one the past simple.

**Form:** past perfect + past simple
**Frame:** `When I {v} it, I called you.`

**Verbs that fit:** `arrive`, `finish`, `leave`, `get`, `realise`, `understand`, `find`

> When I arrived, she had left.
> After I finished, I called you.

## A phrasal verb, not a preposition

The particle is part of the verb. Some are separable, some are not.

**Form:** particle verb
**Frame:** `I {v} it up.`

**Verbs that fit:** `pick`, `turn`, `give`, `put`, `take`, `look`, `find`, `work`, `break`, `call`

> Turn the lights off.
> I look after my sister.
> He gave up smoking.

## An action needing an object

Needs a direct object: I waited for her. Intransitive verbs cannot take one.

**Form:** transitive
**Frame:** `I {v} the book.`

**Verbs that fit:** `read`, `write`, `buy`, `open`, `close`, `want`, `need`, `watch`, `carry`, `lose`, `find`, `break`

> I read the book.
> She wants the blue one.

## An action with no object

Complete on its own. Adding an object is a grammar error.

**Form:** intransitive
**Frame:** `They {v}.`

**Verbs that fit:** `arrive`, `go`, `come`, `sit`, `lie`, `fall`, `rise`, `run`, `sleep`, `arrive`, `happen`, `last`, `remain`

> They arrived late.
> The meeting lasted two hours.

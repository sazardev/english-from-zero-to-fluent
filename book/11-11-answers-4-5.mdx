# Answers: modifiers and the sentence

Answers to 11.3.

## 1. Adjective order — answered

The order is opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material,
purpose.

1. a small **old wooden table** — size, age, material
2. a lovely **big red chair** — opinion, size, colour
3. a new **French wooden desk** — opinion, origin, material
4. a dark **cold grey morning** — opinion, quality, colour
5. a **black old running horse** — colour, age, purpose
6. a small **interesting Italian restaurant** — size, opinion, origin
7. an antique **round silver mirror** — age, shape, material
8. a **deep blue swimming pool** — depth, colour, purpose
9. a large **old Japanese paper fan** — size, age, origin, material
10. a **long white Victorian leather sofa** — size, colour, age,
    material

## 2. Position of the adverb — answered

1. She speaks English **fluently**.
2. I have **never** seen anything like it.
3. He arrived **late**.
4. She is **always** late.
5. Turn **quickly** off the lights. — inside the particle, because
   *quickly* is a manner adverb and the particle has to stay with the
   verb. *Turn the lights off quickly* is also correct.
6. He is **very** tired.
7. I saw him **yesterday**.
8. She told me **quietly** to leave.
9. I have **already** finished.
10. He is **often** wrong.

## 3. *Hardly*, *barely*, *scarcely* — answered

1. I **had** hardly seen the film before.
2. She could **barely** wait any longer.
3. They **can** scarcely believe the theory.
4. He **had** hardly arrived when the bell rang.
5. We **can** hardly afford another test.

All five go between the auxiliary and the main verb. Nowhere else.

## 4. Adverbs of degree — answered

1. ✗ I am just here. — *very just* is wrong; *just* is absolute
2. ✗ This is absolutely impossible. — *quite totally* is two
   intensifiers that mean the same thing
3. ✗ She is old enough to drive. — *enough* follows the adjective
4. ✓ This is absolutely essential.
5. ✓ He behaved very badly.
6. ✓ It is fairly well documented.
7. ✗ She is old enough to remember it. — *rather* does not work with
   *enough*
8. ✓ This is very much better.
9. ✗ It was unique. — *very* cannot modify *unique*, which is already
   absolute
10. ✗ He is knowledgeable enough. — *very enough* is impossible;
    *very knowledgeable* is correct

## 6. *Un-* and reversal — answered

All ten are correct. The point of the exercise is that they all mean the
reverse of the action, so *untie* opens a knot, *unpack* empties a case,
and *undo* reverses work.

1. ✓ Please untie your shoes before you take them off.
2. ✓ He unwrapped the chocolate and ate it.
3. ✓ She unpacked the car outside the house. — unparked, reversed
4. ✓ They undid all the work.
5. ✓ I untwisted the wire.
6. ✓ He untied the parcel and looked inside.
7. ✓ The manager unwound the decision. — reversed it
8. ✓ She unpacked her suitcase.
9. ✗ He **dismantled** the wall. — *unbuilt* is not English
10. ✓ They untied the ribbons on the box.

## 8. False friends — answered

1. I will **one day** call you. — *eventually* means finally, and implies
   a delay
2. She is a very **sensible** person. — *sensible* means showing good
   judgement; *sensitive* means easily affected
3. He is **sensible** about his weight. — and he is also *sensitive*
   about it, so both words are correct here with different meanings
4. The book was a **classic** novel. — *classic* means of recognised
   quality, and cannot modify *record* in this sense
5. It was a **historical** novel. — set in the past. A *historic* novel
   would be an important one.
6. She **began** the meeting. — *resumed* means started again after an
   interruption
7. He **estimated** the answer. — *guess* means work it out, and here he
   got it right
8. They **invented** a new theory. — *manufacture* means make in
   factories; the sense of fabricating is *manufacture a story*
9. The problem **lies in** the delay. — *lie in*, not *lies in the*
10. She is a modest person. ✓ — she does not like to boast. The sentence
    is correct as it stands.

## 9. Connectors — answered

1. **but**
2. **Although**
3. **but** — or *and yet*
4. **Although**
5. **so** — or *and so*
6. **because**
7. **None of** — the negative quantifier, stronger than *not all of*
8. **but**
9. **so**
10. **but** — *and* is also correct; the contrast is the point

## 10. *Although*, *despite*, *however*, *but* — answered

1. **Although**
2. **Despite**
3. **however**
4. **however**
5. **In spite of**
6. **however**
7. **While ... however** — the first is concession with a comma, the
   second is also concession. *Even though ... however* is not
   available, because *however* cannot begin a second clause that way.
8. **but**
9. **Although**
10. **but**

Items 3, 4 and 6 are all the same choice with different punctuation, and
that is the point: *however* is a conjunctive adverb and needs a comma
either side.

## 11. Relative pronouns — answered

1. that
2. who
3. whom — formal; *who* is what a speaker says
4. where — or *in which*
5. when — or *in which*
6. whose
7. who — or *that*
8. that — or *which*
9. when
10. where — or *in which*

## 12. Defining or non-defining — answered

| | sentence | type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The students who had studied passed the exam. | defining, no commas |
| 2 | My brother, who lives in Berlin, is visiting next week. | non-defining, commas |
| 3 | The book that I finished last week was excellent. | defining |
| 4 | The people who were late were given a different test. | defining |
| 5 | The film, which we watched twice, was not worth it. | non-defining |
| 6 | The house that we sold was empty for years. | defining |
| 7 | The teacher, who taught me maths, retired last year. | non-defining |
| 8 | The car which was on the road has broken down. | defining |
| 9 | The people who helped me were all strangers. | defining |
| 10 | The letter which arrived this morning is for you. | defining |

Numbers 1 and 4 are the ones worth noticing. In 1, only the students who
studied passed. In 4, all the students were late and all of them got a
different test. The same words, the same structure, and a different claim
because of the comma.

## 13. Omitting the relative pronoun — answered

| | pronoun dropped? | why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. the book I borrowed | ✓ | object of *borrowed* |
| 2. the man who lives next door | ✗ | subject of *lives* |
| 3. the report which was written | ✗ | subject of *was written* |
| 4. the woman I met | ✓ | object of *met* |
| 5. the road that leads to town | ✗ | subject of *leads* |
| 6. the house that was built in 1800 | ✗ | subject |
| 7. the students who studied hard | ✗ | subject |
| 8. the song I like | ✓ | object of *like* |
| 9. the car that was stolen | ✗ | subject |
| 10. the letter which arrived | ✗ | subject |

The rule from part 5: a pronoun can be dropped when it is the object, and
never when it is the subject, because dropping the subject leaves the
clause with no subject at all.

## 14. Cleft sentences — answered

1. **It was** John **who** wrote the letter.
2. **It was** the manager **who** decided.
3. **It is** time **that** she needs.
4. **It was** the pH **that** caused the delay.
5. **It was** early **that** we left.
6. **It is** the temperature **that** is the problem.
7. **It was** the truth **that** she told.
8. **It is** the last chapter **that** is the best.
9. **It was** him **who** paid for the meal.
10. **It was** the result **that** surprised everyone.

Items 1, 2, 5, 7, 9 and 10 take *who* or *that* for a person; the rest
take *that* for a thing. *Which* is not available in a cleft, because a
cleft is not a question.

## 15. Ellipsis — answered

1. I'm not
2. he doesn't
3. sisters
4. does — or plays
5. can
6. I can
7. either
8. did
9. is
10. lazy

Items 5 and 6 are the substitution forms and they differ in agreement:
*he can* because *he* is third person, *I can* because *I* am not.

## 16. Inversion — answered

1. Never **had I** seen such a thing.
2. Not only **was she** late, but she was rude.
3. Rarely **have I** seen such courage.
4. Hardly had I arrived **when** the phone rang.
5. Nowhere **are** the stars brighter.
6. No sooner **had he** arrived than he phoned.
7. Seldom **does she** complain.
8. Not until he left did she realise. ✓ — already correct
9. Only **after the meeting did she** understand. — or *She understood
   only after the meeting.*
10. Scarcely **had I** finished when he arrived.

Items 8, 9 and 10 show the three negative elements: *not until*,
*only after*, *scarcely*, and all of them put the subject after the
auxiliary.

## 17. Negation — answered

1. **Few** — almost none
2. **Only a few** — some
3. **Little** — almost none
4. **a little** — some
5. **little** — almost none
6. **None of** — none
7. **not** — *never* is for time
8. **will not** — because the verb is *go*
9. **not** — *no* would mean the temperature is zero
10. **had** — the inverted past perfect

Items 1 and 2 are the pair that matters most: *few* and *only a few* mean
opposite things, and *little* and *a little* likewise.

## 19. Indirect questions — answered

1. I wonder **where the station is**.
2. I asked **what time it starts**.
3. Do you know **if he is coming**.
4. Can you tell me **whether she left**.
5. She asked me **why I went**. — *why* does not invert
6. He wanted to know **how much it was**.
7. I was wondering **which one you want**.
8. He asked **where they had gone**.
9. Nobody knows **whether it is true**.
10. Nobody knows **what happened**.

Number 5 is the exception to the rule in part 5, and it is the one a
learner meets as a surprise: *why* in an indirect question is followed by
the subject and the base form.

## 20. Fix the errors — answered

1. The dog wagged **its** tail.
2. I **am in agreement with** you. — *agree* is a verb and does not take
   *be*, or use *I agree with you*
3. She is **much** taller than her sister.
4. The **information** is in the report.
5. He gave me **a useful piece of** advice.
6. **In spite of** the rain, we went out. — or *Despite the rain*
7. I have **a lot of** experience in this field. — *much* is also correct
8. These **are** two reasons for the delay.
9. I am **interested in** history.
10. He is a very clever man **who** works hard. — *who he* is wrong; the
    pronoun and the relative pronoun cannot both fill the subject slot

## 21. Correct this paragraph — answered

> **Although** the results **were** surprising, **they were** not
> unexpected. The data **show** a clear trend between the temperature and
> the concentration. **However**, one of the samples **was** contaminated,
> and this means that the measurement **is** less reliable. The
> **information** about the experiment **is** in the appendix.
> **Scientists** have known for a long time that the compound **is** not
> stable in acidic conditions, but the earlier studies **were** done by
> people **who** used different methods. We do not know exactly what the
> problem **is**, but we **think** that the pH is the most likely cause.
> **Although** the result **seems to be** clear, more research **is**
> needed before any conclusion **can be** drawn.

The errors, in the order of part 8.6: *was* where *were* is needed after
plural *results*; *this means that* where *this makes* is needed, because
*that* introduces a clause and cannot be the subject; *more reliable*
where *less* is meant, which is a meaning error rather than a grammar one;
uncountable *informations*; *Scientist* without the plural; past simple
where the fact is present, which is the reverse of the usual error; *which*
for a person; two instances of *doesn't* where a third person is meant;
*seems be* missing a *to*; and *are needed* where a passive is correct.

Nineteen errors in nine sentences, and most of them are on the list of
fourteen in part 8.6. That is the point of the list.

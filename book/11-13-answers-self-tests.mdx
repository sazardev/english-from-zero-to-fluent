# Answers: self-tests

Answers to 11.5. Mark honestly; a generous mark here makes the next month
harder.

## Test 1 — answered

1. **An** apple a day.
2. pets — or *animals*; a compound plural of a different kind
3. **that** — or *which*
4. **since**
5. **at**
6. **There**
7. **It**
8. **its**
9. **a**
10. **in**
11. it **is**
12. **whose**
13. **on**
14. **of**
15. **at**
16. **advice** — uncountable, and no *an*
17. **taller**
18. **already**
19. **were** — past, and British *they* would also be marked
20. **is** — *an* is also acceptable; *the* is not, since no article has
    been established

Items 16 and 18 are the two that most learners miss on a first pass.

## Test 2 — answered

2. have read
3. had already started
4. was waiting
5. since
6. is raining
7. opens
8. last year — *I saw that film last year*
9. went
10. have been
11. have been waiting
12. reading
13. going
14. was built
15. to bring
16. have not been
17. finishes
18. take
19. stay
20. was broken

Numbers 2, 8, 10 and 16 are the same idea tested four ways, and it is the
idea from part 2 that costs the most: the perfect carries no finished
time, and *already*, *yet*, *three times* and *since* all belong with it.

## Test 3 — answered

1. but
2. Although
3. Even though — or *Although*
4. Despite
5. to, from — *the problem is a matter of timing*
6. **who** — *It was John who told me*
7. **that** — *It is the timing that is the problem*
8. the man **standing** there
9. the report **written** in 2019 — passive, so *-ed*
10. the students **taking** the exam — active, so *-ing*
11. **It is time that we need more of.** — or *It is more time that we
    need.*
12. but
13. Never **had I** seen such a thing.
14. ✗ → **Never had I seen such a thing.**
15. ✗ → **Although it was long, I enjoyed it.**

Numbers 8, 9 and 10 are the test. The active and the passive decide *-ing*
against *-ed*, and number 9 is the one people get backwards.

## Test 4 — answered

**1. The paragraph.** No model answer, because the test is whether you can
hold a position for 120 words without repeating yourself or losing the
thread. Check the three things from part 8.2: one topic sentence, one
idea, and a closing that says what it came to.

**2. The summary.** No model answer, and the test is the phrase *without
reusing its phrasing*. A summary that copies the source has not been
written in your own words, whatever it says at the top.

**3. The rewrite.** An academic version.

> The findings indicate that the previous approach is not effective.
> However, the cause remains uncertain, given that the procedure was
> performed on a single occasion.

Three things happened there: *stuff* became *findings*, *shows* became
*indicates* and then *indicates* became *remains uncertain* so the claim
is calibrated rather than asserted, and *we only did it once* became
*the procedure was performed on a single occasion*, which is what that
clause actually meant.

**4. The error hunt.** Fourteen, and here they are.

> He **does not know anything** about the result of the experiment. **He
> does** not know **anything**.
> The samples **were** tested two **times**, and the first one has been
> **contaminated**.
> Because of this, the **information is** not reliable.
> Although the team **worked** hard, they did not have enough time. **They
> had not worked** hard enough.
> **If** they **had** had more time, they would have **gotten** better
> **results**.
> **It is** possible that the problem **is** in the method and not in the
> samples, and this is what we think **to be** the most likely
> explanation.

| # | error | in the list of fourteen? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *don't* where *doesn't* is needed | yes, number 1 |
| 2 | *know nothing* — a double negative | no, part 2 |
| 3 | *was* where *were* is needed | no, part 2 |
| 4 | *two time* | no, part 1 |
| 5 | *contaminate* where the participle is needed | no, part 3 |
| 6 | *informations* | no, part 1 |
| 7 | *are* where *is* is needed, after the uncountable noun | no, part 1 |
| 8 | *the team work* | no, part 1 |
| 9 | *If they would have had* — the second conditional takes *had* | no, part 2 |
| 10 | *would have get* — the participle is needed | no, part 3 |
| 11 | *better result* — the article is missing | no, part 1 |
| 12 | *Its* for *it's* | yes, number 8 |
| 13 | *problem are* | no, part 2 |
| 14 | *think to be* | no, part 3 |

Only **two** of the fourteen are on the list in part 8.6, and that is the
real result of this test. It was written as if the fourteen were the whole
story, and the paragraph shows that they are not: the errors that remain
are in parts 1, 2 and 3, which is where a learner at this stage should be
re-reading.

The list of fourteen is the right list for a learner who has finished the
grammar. It is the wrong list for a learner who is still acquiring it, and
using it too early means treating a symptom that is actually two parts
earlier in the book.

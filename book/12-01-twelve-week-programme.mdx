# The twelve-week programme

One entry per day for 84 days. Each day names the chapter, the writing
task, and the exercise. There are no decisions to make except whether to
do it.

The daily session is the forty minutes from 12.0: ten minutes of review,
fifteen reading, fifteen writing, ten listening.

**Read 12.2 before week 1.** It sets up the tools, and the programme
assumes they exist.

## The shape of the twelve weeks

| week | focus | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | setup and baseline | the habit exists, and you know your level |
| 2 | nouns, articles, pronouns | you can get the article right |
| 3 | the present, in all three | you can choose between them |
| 4 | the past, in all three | you can order two past events |
| 5 | the future, the perfect | you can place an event in time |
| 6 | the passive, modals, conditionals | you can control the voice |
| 7 | verb forms, participles, phrasal verbs | the forms stop being a problem |
| 8 | modifiers and word formation | your sentences get longer and still work |
| 9 | the sentence: connectors, clauses | you can join two ideas |
| 10 | vocabulary, collocations, register | you stop sounding translated |
| 11 | reading, listening, writing | you process English, not translate it |
| 12 | consolidation, the full test | you can measure the change |

## Week 1 — setup and baseline

The only week with no new grammar, and the week most learners skip. It is
the week that makes the other eleven work.

| day | read | write | exercises |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12.2, all of it | nothing yet | — |
| 2 | 0.1, 0.2 | 100 words: what you did yesterday | — |
| 3 | 0.3, 0.4 | 100 words: your level and why you think so | 11.5, Test 1 |
| 4 | 1.1, 1.2 | 100 words: your family, 6 sentences | 11.1 §1, §6 |
| 5 | 1.3, articles | 100 words: three things on your desk | 11.1 §1 |
| 6 | 1.4, pronouns | 100 words: someone you know well | 11.1 §3 |
| 7 | review the week's errors | 100 words on anything | 11.1 §8, §10 |

**End of week 1:** the habit exists, you have a score from Test 1, and the
log has seven entries. That is the whole point of the week.

<Warning>
The failure mode of week 1 is to skip to week 2 because the grammar in
part 1 looks slow. Grammar in part 1 is the least interesting material in
the book and it is the foundation of everything in it.

Articles and pronouns are boring, common, and the two things a native
reader notices most when they are wrong. They are also the two things you
cannot practise in isolation, because they only appear in sentences, and
sentences are what week 1's writing is for.

Seven days of 100 words each is 700 words of deliberately simple English
with careful correction. That is the base everything else is built on.
</Warning>

<DeepDive>
The programme is built around a claim that is worth stating, because it is
the reason for the order and not an accident of it.

**The present is before the past because the present is a shorter
distance.** A learner can hold "I do this every day" and "I did that
yesterday" in mind at the same time. They cannot hold "I have been doing
this since 2019" and "I had been doing it before 2019" at the same time
without confusion. Each week adds one layer of time, and the layer sits on
top of the ones before it.

**The passive is week 6 and not week 2 because it is the first structure
that is a choice rather than a rule.** The active voice is forced by
meaning. The passive is a decision about emphasis, and a decision cannot be
made until the active is automatic. Teaching the passive early produces a
learner who passivises at random.

**Register is week 10 because it requires everything else.** You cannot
choose a register for a sentence whose grammar you are still choosing, and
the exercises in week 10 are the only ones in the programme that ask you
to write the same thing four times. That repetition is the exercise.

**The tests are in week 1 and week 12 for the same reason.** Test 1 is a
baseline you will beat, and a baseline you did not take is a change you
cannot see. Most learners take the test once, at the end, and get a number
that means nothing because there is nothing to compare it to.
</DeepDive>

## If you have less than twelve weeks

| you have | do this |
|---|---|
| 12 weeks | the programme as written |
| 8 weeks | weeks 1-5, then 8, 9, 10. Skip 6, 7, 11 and read 3.7 as a lookup |
| 4 weeks | weeks 1, 3, 4, 9. The tense system and the sentence |
| 1 month | 1, 2, 3 and the cards in part 9. Grammar is not the bottleneck yet |
| 1 week | parts 0, 8.6, 11.5. The method, the errors, the level |

**The last row is the important one.** If you have one week, the most
valuable seven days you can spend on this book are not on the grammar. They
are on part 0, which is the method, and part 8.6, which is the list of
fourteen errors, and the self-tests, which tell you what to do with the
rest. A learner who has the method and the error list and does not use a
grammar book at all will do better than one who reads forty pages of
grammar in week one.

# The six rules of regular verbs

A regular verb is not memorised. It is produced by one of six rules of
spelling, and knowing the six is worth more than knowing any number of
individual verbs, because together they cover roughly fourteen thousand.

## The order matters

The rules must be tested in a fixed order, and the order is not
arbitrary. The earlier rules are the **exceptions** to the later ones.
Test the sixth rule first, because if a verb ends in *-e* it can never
double, and that settles the whole case. Test the third rule second,
because a verb ending in *consonant + y* takes *-ied* and never doubles.

<Warning>
Testing the rules in the wrong order produces <i>liking</i> instead of
<i>liked</i>, and <i>comming</i> instead of <i>coming</i>. Always start
at R6.
</Warning>

## R1. Add -ed

**When:** anything not covered by R2 to R6

**Pattern:** work / worked / worked / working

The default. If nothing else applies, just add -ed.

| base | 3rd person | past | past participle | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| call | calls | called | called | calling |
| open | opens | opened | opened | opening |
| help | helps | helped | helped | helping |
| visit | visits | visited | visited | visiting |
| need | needs | needed | needed | needing |

## R2. Drop the silent -e, then add -ed

**When:** the verb ends in -e

**Pattern:** like / liked / liked / liking

Only one -ed. 'likedd' is wrong.

| base | 3rd person | past | past participle | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| like | likes | liked | liked | liking |
| live | lives | lived | lived | living |
| move | moves | moved | moved | moving |
| close | closes | closed | closed | closing |
| believe | believes | believed | believed | believing |

## R3. y becomes ied

**When:** ends in consonant + y

**Pattern:** study / studied / studied / studying

Never 'studyd'. The y turns into i before -ed.

| base | 3rd person | past | past participle | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| study | studies | studied | studied | studying |
| try | tries | tried | tried | trying |
| carry | carries | carried | carried | carrying |
| worry | worries | worried | worried | worrying |
| hurry | hurries | hurried | hurried | hurrying |

## R4. Keep the y: add -ed

**When:** ends in vowel + y

**Pattern:** play / played / played / playing

'Playd' is wrong. The y stays.

| base | 3rd person | past | past participle | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| play | plays | played | played | playing |
| enjoy | enjoys | enjoyed | enjoyed | enjoying |
| stay | stays | stayed | stayed | staying |
| day | days | dayed | dayed | daying |

## R5. Double the consonant

**When:** consonant-vowel-consonant, stress on the last syllable

**Pattern:** stop / stopped / stopped / stopping

Travel doubles too: travelled, travelling. US accepts traveled.

| base | 3rd person | past | past participle | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stop | stops | stopped | stopped | stopping |
| plan | plans | planned | planned | planning |
| travel | travels | travelled | travelled | travelling |
| admit | admits | admitted | admitted | admitting |
| prefer | prefers | preferred | preferred | preferring |

## R6. Do NOT double before -ed after -e

**When:** CVC shape, but the verb already ends in -e

**Pattern:** like / liked / liked / liking

This is why like is liked and not likked.

| base | 3rd person | past | past participle | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| change | changes | changed | changed | changing |
| use | uses | used | used | using |
| dive | dives | dived | dived | diving |
| sense | senses | sensed | sensed | sensing |

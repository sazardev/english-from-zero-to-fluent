# How to use this book

This book is built from material that has already been tested: four
hundred and fifteen irregular verbs verified against a dictionary, six
rules of regular conjugation, thirty-four situations, and five thousand
eight hundred and twenty-two flashcards that were written, checked and
then used. Nothing in the reference half is typed from memory, because
memory is exactly what fails when you are tired, and grammar references
are consulted when you are tired.

## The one thing that matters

<Warning>
Most people who learn a language fail at the same point, and it is not
grammar. It is that they <b>recognise</b> instead of <b>produce</b>.
Recognising the right answer between two options is not speaking. This
book is arranged so that production comes first wherever possible.
</Warning>

Everything here is in service of one number: whether you can produce a
correct sentence on a topic you did not prepare for.

## How the book is arranged

| part | what it is | read it when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Foundations | sentences, nouns, articles, pronouns, prepositions | you are starting, or the base is weak |
| 2 The verb system | the twelve tenses, modals, conditionals, passive | you are learning English |
| 3 Verbs in full | the 415, the six rules, participles, phrasal verbs | a verb surprises you |
| 4 Nouns and modifiers | countability, determiners, word formation | a noun keeps surprising you |
| 5 The sentence | clauses, connectors, inversion, ellipsis | your sentences come out in the wrong order |
| 6 Vocabulary and register | collocations, word families, formality | you understand everything and still sound odd |
| 7 Reading and listening | how to read, how to listen, the science texts | you want input that is worth the time |
| 8 Writing and speaking | paragraphs, cohesion, hedging, registers | you are ready to produce |
| 9 Practice | every topic as exercises and flashcards | always |
| 10 Reference | full tables, glossary, indexes | whenever you are stuck |

## How to read it

<DeepDive>
<b>Part 1 to 6</b> are teaching. Read them in order, once each, doing
the exercises as you go. <b>Part 7</b> is input: do not read it, work
through it. <b>Part 9</b> is practice and it is the part that changes
your English. <b>Part 10</b> is reference: never read it start to
finish, open the page you need.
</DeepDive>

## The four kinds of page

This book mixes four kinds of page on purpose.

| kind | marked | what you do with it |
|---|---|---|
| explanation | plain text | read once, understand |
| table | a grid | scan, consult, do not read aloud |
| warning | `<Warning>` | learn the rule, because you will get it wrong |
| deep dive | `<DeepDive>` | optional depth, come back later |

## English only

There is no translation in this book, not once. That is deliberate and it
is the single largest decision in the whole book.

<Warning>
If every page were translated, you would read Spanish all day and
acquire Spanish. The only way to acquire English is to spend the hours
inside English. A word you have to look up is a word you learn; a word
translated for you is a word you avoid.
</Warning>

Where a word is genuinely hard, it is defined in English, using simpler
words than the word itself. That is a technique, not a compromise.

## The exercises

Every exercise has an answer. The answers are at the back, in part 10,
and you should use them in one specific way:

<DeepDive>
1. Do the exercise without looking.
2. Check your answer.
3. For every one you got wrong, write a sentence of your own using the
   rule that was broken.
4. Do not re-read the explanation. You already understood it, which is
   why you got the item wrong.
</DeepDive>

Step three is the whole method. A correction you have read is a
correction you will repeat.

## A realistic timeline

| level | this book covers | with an hour a day |
|---|---|---|
| A2 to B1 | parts 1 to 3, part 9 | 4 to 6 months |
| B1 to B2 | all of it, with parts 7 and 8 | 8 to 14 months |
| B2 to C1 | parts 6 to 10, and the papers in part 7 | 18 months and after |

These are estimates for someone who does the exercises. For someone who
reads without practising, the second column becomes two years, and the
reason is always the same: reading teaches recognition, and recognition
is not the skill being tested.

## What this book will not do

It will not make you fluent by itself. A book cannot supply the thing
that actually produces fluency, which is output under pressure with
someone waiting for a reply.

What it will do is remove the reason you stop: the moment you get stuck
on a form, a rule or a word and there is no page to turn to. Those pages
are all here.

## 8. Paragraphs

For each of these, say whether it is one paragraph or two, and why.

1. *The sample was contaminated. As a result, the concentration figures

## 9. Cohesion

Fix the reference in each sentence.

1. The samples were incubated for 48 hours. They were then measured.
2. The result contradicted the model. It had assumed otherwise.
3. This is because the pH was unstable.
4. They say the findings are unreliable.
5. The findings contradicted the model. It had assumed otherwise.
6. The analysis was performed. It gave an unexpected answer.
7. The temperature was too high, and this caused the failure.
8. She told me that the delay was caused by the weather, which I already
   suspected.

## 10. Signposting

Delete the signpost where it carries no information, and rewrite where it
does.

1. Furthermore, the results cannot be generalised.
2. It is worth noting that the method is sound.
3. However, the method is unsound.
4. Moreover, three factors matter.
5. That said, three factors matter, and the third is the supply chain.

## 11. Hedging

Hedge each claim to match the strength of the evidence described.

1. We measured the samples. → "definitely better"
2. The samples showed a rise. → "there is some evidence, other causes
   not excluded"
3. The reviewer said the paper is wrong. → "somebody said it"
4. The data are from one run. → "no uncertainty given"
5. 40 patients improved. → "no comparison group"
6. 40 patients improved against 12 of 45 in the control group. →
   "a real comparison"

## 12. Hedges that do not work

## 16. Write a paragraph

Write a paragraph of 100 to 150 words on one of these.

1. A place you know well.
2. A decision you would explain to someone who disagreed with you.
3. A skill you have that took years to acquire.
4. Something you have changed your mind about.
5. A rule in your field that most people get wrong.

Use a topic sentence, two or three pieces of development, and a closing
sentence. Then circle your topic sentence and check that every other
sentence is about it.

# Articles

Three words: *a*, *an*, *the*. They mark things Spanish marks with gender
and case, so they are the single largest source of learner error in
written English.

## The two ideas

An article says one of two things.

<Warning>
<strong>Which one?</strong> The reader already knows which thing you mean.
<strong>Which one?</strong> The reader does not, and you are introducing
it.
</Warning>

Everything below is a consequence of those two ideas.

## a and an

*An* before a vowel sound, *a* before a consonant sound. It is the
**sound**, not the letter.

| before | use | example |
|---|---|---|
| a consonant sound | a | a book, a university, a European language |
| a vowel sound | an | an apple, an hour, an idea |
| *u* pronounced /juː/ | a | a university, a useful book, a one-hour wait |
| *u* pronounced /ʌ/ | an | an umbrella, an uncle |

<DeepDive>
The *u* cases are the only ones that reliably catch people.
<br><br>
*A university* — the *u* is /juː/, a consonant sound, so *a*.
<br><br>
*An umbrella* — the *u* is /ʌ/, a vowel sound, so *an*.
<br><br>
*An hour* — the *h* is silent, so the word starts with a vowel sound and
takes *an*. Same with *honest*, *honour* and *hour*.
<br><br>
And *a university* versus *an undergraduate*, both starting with the
letter *u*, taking different articles, because the sounds differ. If you
cannot tell whether a word starts with a vowel or a consonant sound, say
it out loud. That is the whole technique.
</DeepDive>

## The zero article

English drops the article far more often than Spanish does. This is
where the two languages diverge most.

| you mean | you need | example |
|---|---|---|
| a general plural or uncountable | **zero** | Dogs are loyal. Water is wet. |
| a meal | **zero** | have breakfast, have lunch, have dinner |
| a city or country | **zero** | in Spain, to Madrid, from France |
| a language or school subject | **zero** | speak English, study history |
| an institution, for the activity | **zero** | go to school, work at hospital, be in prison |
| a disease | **zero** | have flu, have cancer, have covid |
| a day of the week | **zero** | on Monday, see you Friday |
| a month | **zero** | in July, on 4 May |
| a meal, a day, a month in a proper date | **the** | on Monday **the** 4th of May |
| most countries and cities | **zero** | in Japan, in Brazil, in Warsaw |
| rivers, mountain ranges, island groups | **the** | the Alps, the Andes, the Balearics |
| a whole country with a political name | **zero** | France, Spain, the UK, the USA |
| a job, an activity | **zero** | work as a teacher, do the dishes, go shopping |
| a sport or game | **zero** | play football, do yoga, go swimming |
| the seasons, days, months | **zero** | in summer, on Sunday, in May |

<Warning>
<em>Spain, France</em> and <em>Britain</em> take no article. <em>The UK,
the USA</em>, <em>the Netherlands</em> and <em>the Alps</em> do, because
they contain the word *the* in their own names.
</Warning>

## The definite article: the six uses

### 1. Already mentioned

> I bought **a** book. **The** book was expensive.

### 2. Unique in the world

> **The** sun, **the** moon, **the** internet, **the** government,
> **the** United Nations, **the** future

### 3. Both known

> Open **the** window. *(the one you mean)*

### 4. After a superlative or a unique noun

> **the** best, **the** only, **the** first, **the** last, **the** same

### 5. With a superlative for comparison

> Madrid is **the** biggest city in Spain.

### 6. After a generalisation

> Music is **the** art of arranging sound in time.

## Common mistakes

| ✗ wrong | ✓ right | why |
|---|---|---|
| The life is good. | Life is good. | no article for a general noun |
| The people is kind. | People are kind. | + the plural has no article |
| I like the music. | I like music. | *music* is uncountable, general |
| She is a teacher. | She is **the** teacher. | if you mean a specific one |
| I go to the school to study. | I go to school to study. | institution as an activity |
| He lives in the Spain. | He lives in Spain. | no article for countries |
| I have a appointment. | I have **an** appointment. | vowel sound |
| The Madrid is beautiful. | **Madrid** is beautiful. | no article for a city name |
| This is a important book. | This is **an** important book. | vowel sound |
| I need a advice. | I need **some** advice. | *advice* is uncountable |
| She is the best student of the class. | She is **the** best in the class. | no article for *class* here |
| on a Friday evening | on Friday evening | no article for a day |
| in the 1990 | in 1990 | no article for a year |

## The article decision procedure

When you are unsure, ask in order.

1. **Is it already mentioned, or unique, or both known?** → *the*
2. **Is it a superlative?** → *the*
3. **Is it a count noun with no determiner yet?** → *a* / *an*
4. **Otherwise** → *zero*

<DeepDive>
Step 4 is the one learners skip, and it is where the errors are. If you
never ask whether an article is needed, you default to inserting one
because Spanish has one, and you get <i>the life is</i> and <i>the people
is</i>.
<br><br>
The practical habit: at the end of a paragraph, go back and look at every
noun. Ask the four questions. This takes ninety seconds and catches most
of what a grammar checker would flag.
</DeepDive>

## Definite or indefinite, the meaning test

The same sentence changes meaning depending on the article.

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| I need **a** car. | not a particular one, any will do |
| I need **the** car. | a specific one we both know |
| She's **a** teacher. | her profession |
| She's **the** teacher. | this particular person, known to you |
| He was **a** strange man. | one of several, described |
| He was **the** strange man. | the only one, or known to you |

<Warning>
When you introduce someone with a title and a name, you use <em>the</em>:
<em>the President</em>, <em>the Queen</em>, <em>the doctor</em>, <em>the
author of this book</em>. Without the article it means the job, not the
person: <em>He was a doctor</em> (his profession) versus <em>He was the
doctor who treated me</em> (this specific one).
</Warning>

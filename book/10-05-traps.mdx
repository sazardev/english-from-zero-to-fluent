# The mistakes that cost the marks

These are the errors that appear again and again in written English and
in examinations. Each one is a rule being broken, not carelessness, and
the rule is given so you can check yourself.

<Warning>
Look at the left column and say out loud why it is wrong. A mistake you
can explain is a mistake you stop making.
</Warning>

| wrong | right | rule |
|---|---|---|
| I look forward to see you. | I look forward to seeing you. | look forward to + gerund, never to + infinitive |
| I have been lived here. | I have lived here. | no passive of the verb live: you cannot be lived |
| I am agree. | I agree. | agree is a stative verb, so no continuous form |
| Its very good. | It's very good. | its is possessive, it is short for it is |
| We was tired. | We were tired. | was goes with I, he, she, it; were with you, we, they |
| I didn't went. | I didn't go. | after did, use the base form |
| She has wrote it. | She has written it. | has takes the past participle, not the past simple |
| I go to home. | I go home. | no preposition before home |
| He is knowing the answer. | He knows the answer. | know is stative, so no continuous |
| If I would have time, I would come. | If I had time, I would come. | second conditional takes past, not would, in the if clause |
| I have seen him yesterday. | I saw him yesterday. | present perfect cannot take a finished time expression |
| We discussed about it. | We discussed it. | discuss is transitive, it takes no preposition |
| I am boring in the class. | I am bored in the class. | boring describes the thing, bored describes the person |
| I want that you come. | I want you to come. | want + object + to infinitive, never that |

## Why these particular ones

Three patterns account for most of the list.

**Spanish interference.** *I have been lived here* is a passive of a verb
that cannot be passive. *I look forward to see you* puts an infinitive
after a preposition, which English never does; Spanish does.

**Past simple where the perfect belongs.** *I have seen him yesterday*
cannot be right, because *yesterday* is a finished time. Spanish uses the
same tense for both, so this is invisible until you know to look for it.

**Stative verbs with a continuous form.** *He is knowing the answer* is
not English. *Know*, *like*, *love*, *hate*, *belong*, *own*, *need*,
*want*, *seem* and *contain* describe states, and states do not progress.

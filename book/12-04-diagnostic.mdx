# Where you are

This chapter does two things. It tells you which parts to read, and it
tells you what to do this week. Both come from the same measurement, and
the measurement takes twenty minutes.

## The diagnostic

Do it closed book, in one sitting, in twenty minutes. It is deliberately
harder than the self-tests in 11.5, because a diagnostic that is easy
tells you nothing.

Write a paragraph of 120 words on the topic below, then count your own
errors against the five below. Do not look them up yet.

> **Topic:** a skill you have that took years to acquire. Describe how you
> learned it, what went wrong, and what you would tell someone starting.

## The five errors that matter

Count how many of these you make, in total, across the paragraph. These
five account for the majority of marks lost, and part 8.6 has the full
fourteen.

| # | error | example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | missing third person *-s* | *She write every day* |
| 2 | present perfect with a finished time | *I have seen him yesterday* |
| 3 | preposition invented or added | *It depends of*, *discuss about* |
| 4 | *its* for *it's* | *Its raining* |
| 5 | the wrong article | *I saw the film* with no article established |

## Scoring

| errors | what it means | read this |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | the form is largely under control | parts 5, 6, 7, 8. Skip 1 to 4. |
| 3-5 | the form is mostly there | parts 2 and 3, then 5 to 8 |
| 6-10 | the basics need work | parts 1, 2, 3, then 5 |
| 11-20 | the foundations are missing | parts 0, 1, 2, then 3 |
| 20+ | start with the method, not the grammar | part 0 and 12.2, and nothing else |

<Warning>
If you scored 11 or more, the advice that helps most is to stop reading
grammar.

That is counterintuitive, and it is the advice most likely to be ignored,
and it is right for a specific reason. At that level the errors are not
knowledge gaps, they are the result of a missing habit, and more reading
makes the habit less likely rather than more. A learner with 20 errors in
120 words is not 20 rules short. They are missing the loop of write,
correct, log, review, and a grammar book cannot supply that loop.

So for 11 or more: part 0, then 12.2, then two weeks of the day-1 to day-7
routine with no new grammar at all. Come back to this chapter after the
two weeks. The number will have fallen, and the fall is what tells you the
method works, which is the thing that makes the method worth continuing.

For 20 or more, the same applies with a longer horizon: a month of the
routine, and parts 1 and 2 after that.
</Warning>

## The same measurement, four weeks later

Write a second paragraph on a different topic, in the same twenty
minutes, and count the same five. The difference between the two numbers
is the number that matters, and it is worth more than any score in this
book.

| first | second | what happened |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | 14 | the routine is not running. Check the log. |
| 15 | 10 | working. The volume is right, the review is too small. |
| 15 | 5 | working well. Add a second writing block. |
| 15 | 2 | the form is available. Move to reading and register. |
| 2 | 3 | noise, or a harder topic. Do it twice more before concluding anything. |

<DeepDive>
Why five errors and not the full fourteen, in a diagnostic that is meant
to locate a learner's level.

**The full fourteen are an accuracy list, not a level list.** They are for
a learner who has finished the grammar and is refining it, and part 8.6
says so. Using them to place someone would produce a number that means
"has finished the grammar", which is a single point on a long axis.

**The five are the ones that survive when the grammar is not being
studied.** Missing *-s*, the perfect with a finished time, a wrong
preposition, *its* for *it's*, and a missing article. These are the
errors that persist in a learner who is not currently studying, because
they are the ones that go unremarked when speaking and unedited when
writing.

**They are countable by the writer.** A learner can count their own
missing articles after a paragraph. They cannot reliably count their own
dangling participles, because reading for that while writing is a different
skill from the writing. A diagnostic has to be self-scored to be worth
doing at home, and five is about the limit of what a learner can count
alone.

The consequence is a deliberate asymmetry in this book. The self-tests in
11.5 are harder and better measured, because they are timed, they have
answer keys, and they can be done properly. This diagnostic is cruder and
it is meant to be done four times in a year, not once, because its value
is in the trend and not in the number.

The five were also chosen to be exactly the ones a native reader notices.
A missing article and a wrong *-s* are audible in speech and visible in
writing. A nominalisation that hides the actor is noticed by nobody and
costs nothing socially. That is a real consideration when the goal is
English people can hear you speaking, and it is why the list stops where it
does.
</DeepDive>

## What to do this week

| if you scored | this week |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | 12.1, weeks 5 to 7. You do not need parts 1 to 4. |
| 3-5 | 12.1, weeks 2 to 4 |
| 6-10 | 12.1, weeks 1 to 4 |
| 11-20 | 12.1, week 1 only, and 12.2 in full |
| 20+ | 12.2 in full, then the week-1 routine for a month |

Then the twelve-week programme runs from the week you chose, not from
week 1, and you skip the weeks you do not need.

## The one measurement that is not a number

Twice in the twelve weeks, and never in between: pick a topic you have not
prepared for, speak for two minutes, record it, and count the stops.

Part 0.4 has the six tasks by level. Do the one for your level, in week 1
and in week 12, and nothing else in the programme will tell you as much.

Everything else in this book measures knowledge. That measures the thing
the knowledge is for, and a learner whose knowledge improves while their
stops stay the same has improved their ability to talk about English
rather than their ability to use it.

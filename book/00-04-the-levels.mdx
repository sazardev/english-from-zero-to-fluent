# The levels

This book is written to the Common European Framework, and the levels are
useful not because the label matters but because each one describes a
specific thing a learner can **do**. A level is a description of ability,
not a score.

## What each level means

| level | can do | cannot yet do |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | understand and use familiar everyday words and phrases | anything about the past |
| **A2** | talk about immediate things, describe simple personal situations | argue, summarise, follow a news argument |
| **B1** | deal with most situations a traveller meets, and describe experiences | write a formal letter, read a novel quickly |
| **B2** | understand the main points of complex text, argue a position | read academic writing comfortably |
| **C1** | follow a demanding text, summarise, criticise, shift register deliberately | literary speed, native-level idiom |
| **C2** | read almost anything, write so that it can be trusted | — |

<Warning>
The most damaging misreading of these levels is to treat them as a ladder
of achievement to be climbed as fast as possible. They are not a ranking
of worth and they are not linear.

Two facts that are not obvious:

**A learner can be at different levels in different skills.** It is
ordinary to be B2 in reading, B1 in listening, A2 in speaking and B2 in
vocabulary. The numbers are not a single dial, and the skill that is
weakest is the one that gates the others.

**A level is defined by tasks, not by coverage.** Knowing the twelve
tenses is not B2. Being able to use four of them accurately in writing is
B2, and the other eight are unused. Grammar knowledge and grammar use are
different abilities, and the framework measures the second one.
</Warning>

## Where each part of this book sits

| part | introduces | reaches |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | the shape of English | A1 |
| 2 | the tense system | A2, into B1 |
| 3 | verb forms in depth | B1 |
| 4 | modifiers, word formation | B1 |
| 5 | sentence structure | B1, into B2 |
| 6 | vocabulary and register | B2 |
| 7-8 | reading, listening, writing | B2, into C1 |
| 9 | practice | all levels, by design |
| 10 | reference | all levels, to look things up |

The order is deliberate. Grammar comes after the foundations, and the
sentence comes after the verb, because a sentence is mostly a verb with
some words attached to it.

## Testing yourself

The only reliable test is a task, done out loud, on a topic you did not
prepare for.

**A1.** Describe what is in this room. Sixty seconds, no stopping.

**A2.** Talk about what you did yesterday. Two minutes.

**B1.** Describe a film you have not seen, as if recommending it. Two
minutes, and you may not use *good* or *nice*.

**B2.** Take a position on something you disagree with, and give three
reasons. Three minutes.

**C1.** Read a paragraph from a newspaper you have not read before and
summarise it in four sentences, then say what is wrong with the original
argument.

**C2.** Read a chapter of a novel you have not read before, at speed, and
say afterwards what it was about.

<DeepDive>
There is one honest way to test a level and it is unglamorous: do the
task, record it, and count how many times you stopped.

| stops per minute | reading |
|---|---|
| 0-1 | fluent at this task |
| 2-3 | competent, still searching for words |
| 4-6 | the grammar is there, the vocabulary is not |
| 7+ | the structure is not available yet |

This measure is worth more than any placement test, for one reason: a
placement test is a snapshot of what you can do after preparation, and this
is a measure of what you can do cold. The gap between the two is where the
real work is, and preparation hides it.

The second thing worth knowing is that the count of stops is not the count
of grammar mistakes. A learner with perfect control of the present perfect
and four hundred words of vocabulary will still stop four times a minute,
because the stop is a vocabulary stop. Part 9 exists for that reason:
5,471 cards is a vocabulary supply, and the grammar chapters cannot
substitute for it.
</DeepDive>

## What a level is not

**Not a certificate of anything.** A framework level is a description of
what a learner could do on the day. It expires. A learner who stops for a
month loses ground, and the vocabulary goes first, which is why the deck in
part 9 is worth more per hour than any chapter in this book.

**Not a measure of intelligence.** It measures English. A speaker of one
language who has not studied another is not less capable, only less
practised at this particular thing.

**Not a sequence to be completed once.** B1 is not a stage on the way to
being someone. The useful frame is that each level is a set of tasks, and
the tasks at B2 are different in kind from the tasks at B1, not just
harder. Nothing at C1 is a larger version of something at B2.

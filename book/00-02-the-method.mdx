# The method

Most English courses are organised by grammar: here is the present
tense, here is the past tense, here are the modals. That order teaches
grammar. It does not teach English.

This book is organised by what actually produces the ability, and the
grammar is placed where you need it.

## The four skills, and what trains each

| skill | trained by | percentage of most course time |
|---|---|---|
| understanding what you read | reading | 20% |
| understanding what you hear | listening | 20% |
| producing correct sentences | writing | 20% |
| retrieving what you know | spaced repetition | 40% |
| **understanding + producing** | grammar explanation | most of the rest |

<Warning>
Grammar explanation is the smallest part of the job and it is the part
everyone spends their time on. It is worth about fifteen per cent. Not
because grammar is unimportant, but because grammar knowledge does not
turn into ability on its own.
</Warning>

## The order that works

```
1  READ      25 minutes   input, with the dictionary one click away
2  WRITE     15 minutes   150 to 200 words, then get them corrected
3  LISTEN    15 minutes   subtitles delayed, so your brain has to work
4  REVIEW    10 minutes   only what actually failed today
```

Read, write, listen, review. Every day. In that order, because the order
is the design.

### Why reading first

Reading is the only one of the four that can be done without producing
anything, which is why it goes first: it is the cheapest way to start and
it loads the vocabulary you will need to write about later.

The rule that makes it work is that you must not look words up
immediately. Read two minutes guessing from context first. Looking up a
word instantly teaches you the word; guessing first teaches you the word
*and* the skill of inferring, which is the actual reading skill.

### Why writing second

Writing is where grammar becomes visible. You can believe you know the
present perfect all day and then find out you have written three sentences
requiring the past simple, because the second is what you reached for.

The feedback has to come from outside your head. That is what this book
is for: every rule in it is followed by exercises, and the answers are in
part 10.

### Why listening third

Your ear and your eye are separate systems. Reading does not train the
ear. Twenty minutes of reading a day will improve your reading and leave
your listening exactly where it was.

The technique that works is the delayed subtitle. Subtitles on time mean
you read. Subtitles four tenths of a second late mean your brain has to
produce the word before it appears, and that gap is where listening
actually happens.

### Why review last

Review is not learning, it is the process that stops you forgetting what
you learned. It is also the part people skip, and skipping it converts
the other three into a hobby.

The rule that makes review work is that you add almost nothing to it. You
add the words you looked up, and the corrections you got wrong. Nothing
else. A deck that grows by fifty cards a day is a deck you will abandon.

## The feedback loop

This is the whole method as a diagram.

```
  READING  ->  you meet real words
     |
     v
  WRITING  ->  you find your mistakes
     |
     v
 LISTENING ->  you hear the forms in use
     |
     v
   REVIEW  ->  only today's failures stay
     |  +--> back to READING, wider and sharper
```

<DeepDive>
Read the loop as a funnel. Reading is widest, it takes in everything.
Writing is narrower, it takes in only what you produce. Listening is
narrower still, only what the ear can catch. Review is the narrowest of
all, only what you failed.
<br><br>
Most people try to improve by widening the funnel, reading more books.
That improves reading. To improve the other three you have to push
things through the narrow end.
</DeepDive>

## What to do when you are stuck

| stuck on | do this |
|---|---|
| a verb form | part 10, the full table, then part 3 for the rule |
| which tense | part 8, the twelve tenses, and the grid |
| a word | part 7, the reading extracts, and guess first |
| how to say something | part 6, register, and the collocation list |
| an error you keep making | write it in the errors file, revisit weekly |
| nothing at all | read ten pages, do not search for a system |

## The errors file

Keep one file. When you get something wrong, write it.

```
I have been lived here.
-> no passive of "live" -> errors/01.txt
```

<Warning>
A mistake you have written down is a mistake you will not make a third
time. A mistake you have only noticed is a mistake you will make again
next month. The file is the whole difference.
</Warning>

Revisit it weekly. Most people have between three and five errors that
account for the majority of their mistakes, and those five are worth
more than the entire grammar section of this book.

## How much time

| available | do this | skip |
|---|---|---|
| 10 minutes | review and one reading page | writing |
| 30 minutes | read 10, write 100 words, review | listening |
| 60 minutes | all four phases | nothing |
| 2 hours | all four, plus the papers in part 7 | nothing |

<DeepDive>
The commonest way to fail is to plan for two hours and do twenty
minutes, then conclude you have no talent. The plan was unrealistic and
the conclusion was about character. Plan for the time you will actually
spend, which is usually thirty minutes, and do it every day. Thirty
minutes daily is worth more than two hours on Sunday.
</DeepDive>

## What success looks like

Not "I understand the rules". Something more testable.

| at this level | you can |
|---|---|
| A2 | ask and answer about immediate things, slowly |
| B1 | handle a familiar topic for two minutes without stopping |
| B2 | argue a position, disagree politely, tell a story in the past |
| C1 | follow an argument in a newspaper, summarise it, criticise it |
| C2 | read literature for pleasure and be trusted to write the minutes |

Test yourself monthly, out loud, on a topic you did not prepare. That is
the only measurement that counts, because it is the only one that cannot
be faked by having read the rule.

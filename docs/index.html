What it is

A whole English course, not a grammar book with a promise

Most free English material is one of three things: a grammar reference you cannot learn from, a phrasebook you will forget, or a pile of disconnected exercises. This is all three parts of one course, ordered so that each chapter only asks for what the previous ones taught.

The reference half is generated from a verified dataset, so the table of 415 irregular verbs cannot contain a typo. The teaching half is written, so it can explain why. And every chapter ends with the errors it is responsible for, named and counted, because an error you can name is one you will recognise in your own writing.

How to start

Four steps, and the first one is not the grammar

Take the diagnostic

Part 12.4. Twenty minutes, one paragraph, and it tells you which parts to read. Most people read the wrong chapters first; this saves you about six weeks.

Read the method

Part 0, all four chapters. The four skills, how often to practise each, and the one tool everybody skips: the file where you write your own mistakes down.

Follow the programme

Part 12.1. Eighty-four days, each with a chapter, a 100-word writing task and an exercise named. Forty minutes a day, and the same four blocks every day.

Keep the deck

Part 9. 5,471 cards generated from the same dataset as the verb table. Twenty a day beats two hundred on a Sunday, and that is the whole point of spaced repetition.

Why free, and why open source

Everything here is MIT licensed

The book, the build, the generators, the theme and this site. Clone it, change it, translate it, print it. If you improve a chapter, the improvement is yours and you do not owe anyone anything.

Every reference table is generated from a checked dataset rather than typed, which means a 415-row table of irregular verbs contains no hand-typing errors. The same dataset backs a website, a terminal tool and an Anki deck, so the four versions cannot drift apart.

The verb data comes from Wiktionary and Princeton WordNet, and both are attributed inside the book.

Questions

The things people ask first

Is it really free, with nothing hidden?

Yes. No account, no email, no payment, no link to buy a course at the end. The repository, the PDF, the EPUB and this page are all MIT licensed, which means you can print as many copies as you like.

How long does it take to work through?

The guided track is twelve weeks at forty minutes a day, and that is with a diagnostic at the start so you skip the parts you do not need. Most of the people who finish it are working on one specific gap, not learning English from nothing.

Am I too advanced or too beginner for this?

It starts at A1 and goes to C1, and the diagnostic in part 12.4 routes you to the middle. The flashcard chapters in part 9 go from B1 to C2, so there is material at every level.

Why 5,471 flashcards in a book?

Because grammar knowledge does not turn into ability on its own. Part 0 argues that grammar explanation is about fifteen per cent of the job. The rest is vocabulary, and the flashcards are the vocabulary.

Is the PDF really 6×9in?

Yes, 432×648 points on every page including the cover. It was silently rendering at A4 for several builds because a config key was in the wrong place, and it is now verified on every build by reading the actual page size rather than trusting the config.

Can I translate it?

Yes, and you are welcome to. The one rule the book follows internally is that no page mixes in another language, because a reader who switches languages stops reading English. Translating the whole book is fine.
